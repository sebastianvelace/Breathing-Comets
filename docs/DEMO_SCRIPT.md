## Scientific wording corrections

- **“Search anything” → “A target or sky coordinates.”** Name resolution and
  moving-object support have explicit limits; a general comet search is not a
  promise of an automatically tracked image sequence.
- **“102 colors” → “Measured infrared spectrum” for the click scene.** SPHEREx’s
  survey has 102 spectral channels, but the available measurements for a selected
  position depend on observations and quality cuts. Do not imply every click
  returns 102 independent measured points.
- **“20 comets, with measured carbon dioxide” → “20-comet atlas · detections and
  caveats.”** The census includes systematic and marginal cases; preserve their
  distinctions rather than presenting all entries as equally secure detections.
- **“Any coordinates. Any time.” → “Real observations. Open to everyone.”** Users
  can enter coordinates, but image dates and usable coverage depend on the
  released archive and detector. An arbitrary date is not available on demand.
- **“Asteroid sizes, checked against NEOWISE” remains defensible**, provided the
  displayed comparison retains uncertainty and does not present the calibrated
  median as independent accuracy or claim a confirmed hazard prediction.

## What the revision supports in the rubric

**Relevance:** the opening names the actual challenge, then demonstrates coordinate
entry and a time sequence of the same field. **Validity:** real observations,
matched wavelengths, measured spectra, uncertainties and comparison data remain
visible. **Creativity:** the time-domain viewer leads naturally into the comet and
asteroid interpretation. **Impact:** the audience and public, bilingual access
are explicit, without inventing user-study outcomes. **Presentation:** one journey
replaces a rushed feature list, with identity at the start and a useful invitation
at the end.
